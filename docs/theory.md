# Theory section draft

## Setting

Let \(\mathcal{A}\) and \(\mathcal{U}\) be Banach spaces of input and output fields. A PDE family induces a solution operator

\[
\mathcal{S}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{U}.
\]

Let \(G\) be a Lie group acting on the full problem family through actions \(T_g^{\mathcal A}:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A\) and \(T_g^{\mathcal U}:\mathcal U\to\mathcal U\). The problem family is closed under \(G\) when the differential equation, coefficient fields, forcing terms, boundary or initial conditions, domain class, and observation map transform consistently. Under this condition, the exact solution operator is equivariant:

\[
\mathcal{S}(T_g^{\mathcal A}a)=T_g^{\mathcal U}\mathcal{S}(a),\qquad g\in G.
\]

We approximate \(\mathcal{S}\) with a neural operator \(\mathcal{G}_\theta\). The operator-level equivariance defect at \((a,g)\) is

\[
\Delta_\theta(a,g)
=
\mathcal{G}_\theta(T_g^{\mathcal A}a)-T_g^{\mathcal U}\mathcal{G}_\theta(a).
\]

## Local orbit consistency

Let \(\mathfrak g\) be the Lie algebra of \(G\), with generators \(X_1,\ldots,X_m\). For a sampled generator \(X_i\) and scalar \(\epsilon\), define \(g=\exp(\epsilon X_i)\). The proposed regularizer is

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{orb}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{a,i,\epsilon}
\left[
\frac{
\|\Delta_\theta(a,\exp(\epsilon X_i))\|_{\mathcal U}^2
}{\epsilon^2+\eta}
\right].
\]

For a minibatch implementation, \(X_i\) and \(\epsilon\) are sampled stochastically. The same objective can be evaluated without labels because both terms use model predictions and known group actions.

## Proposition 1: finite orbit loss estimates the infinitesimal equivariance defect

Assume \(T_{\exp(\epsilon X)}^{\mathcal A}\), \(T_{\exp(\epsilon X)}^{\mathcal U}\), and \(\mathcal{G}_\theta\) are differentiable at \(\epsilon=0\). Let \(X^{\mathcal A}a\) and \(X^{\mathcal U}u\) denote the infinitesimal actions on inputs and outputs. Then

\[
\mathcal{G}_\theta(T_{\exp(\epsilon X)}^{\mathcal A}a)
-
T_{\exp(\epsilon X)}^{\mathcal U}\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)
=
\epsilon
\left(
D\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)[X^{\mathcal A}a]
-
X^{\mathcal U}\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)
\right)
+O(\epsilon^2).
\]

Therefore, for small \(|\epsilon|\),

\[
\frac{
\|\Delta_\theta(a,\exp(\epsilon X))\|_{\mathcal U}^2
}{\epsilon^2}
\to
\left\|
D\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)[X^{\mathcal A}a]
-
X^{\mathcal U}\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)
\right\|_{\mathcal U}^2.
\]

This is the Lie-algebraic equivariance defect. The finite-difference form avoids explicit Jacobian-vector products.

## Proposition 2: orbit OOD error decomposition

Assume the true solution operator is equivariant and \(T_g^{\mathcal U}\) is bounded. For any \(a\in\mathcal A\) and \(g\in G\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathcal{G}_\theta(T_g^{\mathcal A}a)-\mathcal{S}(T_g^{\mathcal A}a)\|_{\mathcal U}
&\le
\|\mathcal{G}_\theta(T_g^{\mathcal A}a)-T_g^{\mathcal U}\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)\|_{\mathcal U}
\\
&\quad+
\|T_g^{\mathcal U}\|\,
\|\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)-\mathcal{S}(a)\|_{\mathcal U}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Proof:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{G}_\theta(T_g^{\mathcal A}a)-\mathcal{S}(T_g^{\mathcal A}a)
&=
\mathcal{G}_\theta(T_g^{\mathcal A}a)-T_g^{\mathcal U}\mathcal{S}(a)
\\
&=
\left[\mathcal{G}_\theta(T_g^{\mathcal A}a)-T_g^{\mathcal U}\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)\right]
+
T_g^{\mathcal U}\left[\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)-\mathcal{S}(a)\right].
\end{aligned}
\]

Apply the triangle inequality and boundedness of \(T_g^{\mathcal U}\). This decomposition shows that error on symmetry-induced OOD inputs is controlled by the in-distribution prediction error and the learned equivariance defect.

## Proposition 3: connected-group consequence

Suppose \(G\) is connected and generated by \(X_1,\ldots,X_m\). If \(\mathcal{G}_\theta\) is smooth and the infinitesimal equivariance defect vanishes for every generator along the relevant orbit, then \(\mathcal{G}_\theta\) is equivariant on that connected orbit.

Sketch: define

\[
F(t)=T_{\exp(-tX)}^{\mathcal U}\mathcal{G}_\theta(T_{\exp(tX)}^{\mathcal A}a).
\]

The derivative \(F'(t)\) is zero when the infinitesimal defect vanishes along the orbit. Hence \(F(t)=F(0)\) along each one-parameter subgroup. Concatenating flows of generators gives equivariance on the connected component generated by them.

## Approximate and partially broken symmetries

When the symmetry is exact only on a valid output region, replace the norm by a weighted norm:

\[
\|v\|_{w}^{2}=\|w_{a,g}\odot v\|^2.
\]

The corresponding objective is

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{orb}}^w(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{a,i,\epsilon}
\left[
\frac{
\|w_{a,g}\odot\Delta_\theta(a,\exp(\epsilon X_i))\|^2
}{\epsilon^2+\eta}
\right].
\]

This covers boundary masks, valid-overlap masks after transformations, observation masks, and regions where coefficients or forcing fields remain closed under the transformation.

## Computational proposition

For one sampled transform per minibatch, the training computation requires:

1. one forward pass for \(\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)\);
2. one transformed forward pass for \(\mathcal{G}_\theta(T_g^{\mathcal A}a)\);
3. one known output transformation \(T_g^{\mathcal U}\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)\).

Thus the leading training cost is approximately two forward passes plus one backward pass through the combined graph. Inference uses only \(\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)\), so latency and deployment are unchanged relative to the base neural operator.
